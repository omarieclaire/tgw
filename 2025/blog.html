2025 Toronto Games Week Recap

2025 was the biggest year yet for TGW, with 4200 attendees and almost 40 events over seven magical days.



At Toronto Games Week 2025, you could:

  • Connect with queer joy and rage in DEI or DIE Variety Show!
  • Scour High Park for fungi and butterflies in scavenger hunts!
  • Wheel down dark forest paths to the Night Parkcade!
  • Level up your industry knowledge and contacts!
  • Play massive videogames projected on concrete silos at the waterfront!

Special guests

  • Sherwin Tjia (You Are a Cat, Strip Spelling Bee), choose-your-own-adventure creator and quirky event organizer from Montreal who brought the contemplative SUNCHASER flight simulator experience!
  • Paolo Pedercini (Every Day the Same Dream, New York Times Simulator), political game designer and DIY curator from Pittsburgh!
  • Terry Cavanagh (Super Hexagon, VVVVVV), wildly prolific game designer and IndieCade award winner from Ireland!

Thursday, June 12: Kicking Off in Style

We kicked off Toronto Games Week with Giant Video Games at the waterfront. We had massive playable projections lighting up the historic Canada Malting Silos.

Players were selected through a contest involving giant golden tickets.

Mayor Olivia Chow joined in on the fun!

(photos: Yevi Kaliuzhka)

XP Game Summit brought hundreds of professionals together for insights and connections across two days. Behaviour Interactive hosted a meet & greet at their new Toronto office.

(photo: Ryan of XP Summit)

At InterAccess, visitors could experience xXx_FANTASYLAND_xXx a transgressive multimedia project combining videogames, live performance, and projection mapping to explore identity and representation.

(photo: Liam Mackenzie)

Tanya Kan from Vivid Foundry hosted Journaling for Game Makers at Fort York Library, and Mathew Iantorno introduced Spellbinder, a tabletop game that turns books into spellcasting components through Making + Playing a Little Free Library RPG

(photo: Aariz Abdul)

Friday, June 13: Community and Connection

Friday brought the DEI or DIE Variety Showcase & Queer Social to TMU's eighth floor, where hosts Gav Sarafian and Hannah Beatty connected the community through performances, conversations, and gameplay that celebrated queer joy and rage.

(photo: jcallejasrodriguez)

The G.R.I.D. made space for playtesting for delighted devs at George Brown College.

(photo: Mega Power Games)

Saturday, June 14: Adventure Central

On Saturday, The Hunt for the Brightwing Butterfly led participants through High Park on an immersive scavenger hunt.

(photo: jesse of lockwoodimmersive)
Meanwhile the Rat Race | Adventure in the P.A.T.H. challenged teams to explore North America's largest underground pedestrian network. (photo: andrew.k.balram)

In Search of the Godmama offered an alternative ritual for exchanging goddesses from different cultures.

(photo: Ruixuan Li)

Haggle on the Grange turned the park into a large-scale negotiation game.


(photo: Mikhail Honoridez )

The Kitten Cup Tea Party returned to Grange Park with a capybara theme, with tea-posing traditions and indie game demos.

(photo: jsajm quino)

We had a wealth of Game Jam Day events, with Board Game and PICO-8 MicroJams, featuring workshops by Andrew Tran and Adam Saltsman from Finji, followed by "What's Next, Jammer?", a discussion about the cultural and economic value of game jams.


The Pixel Power Play showcase at Red Bull/Overactive Media gave attendees early access to upcoming games.


(photo: Marcel Dee)

Vector Festival's Game Residency Keynote at InterAccess featured documentarian Min Sook Lee discussing autofiction in videogames.

We closed the night with the Night Bike Parkcade, a pop-up arcade with a two-wheeled theme featuring bike-related videogames, protest games, and secret clues to find us.

(photo: Tate Young)

Sunday, June 15: Making and Playing


Sunday opened with hands-on creation at Games Made Open at TTL Makerspace, where participants could hack with open-source tools like Godot and create their own Gameboy cartridges.

(photo: Marie LeBlanc Flanagan)

The Board Game Design Jam at Snakes & Lattes College invited teams to prototype boardgames in a day.


(photo: Andy Phamm)

High Park hosted Metazooa Live!, an evolution-themed scavenger hunt using phones to track plants and animals while building a tree of life.


(photo: Abraham Train)

The Out of Bounds 2ronto speedrunning event at Society Clubhouse showcased women and femme players.


(photo: Marisa Ranalli)

At Hideaway Studios, Dream Game Incorporated, brought participants' drawings to life in a playable game prototype with live musical accompaniment.

(photo: Caitlin Russell)

The Indie Game Emporium at Houndstooth offered a chance to meet local developers and purchase zines, diy games, and other game-art objects.


(photo: Andy Phamm)

The day wrapped with experimental performances at Play House at See-Scape, with live electronic music and games, and then the NextGen Showcase at CSI Spadina, featuring curated games from Toronto's emerging talent.

(photo: Kevin Ly)

Monday, June 16: Building Community

On Monday we had Deck Building at Lillian H. Smith Library where participants created trading cards.

(photo: Marie LeBlanc Flanagan)

The Playful Page conversation at Palmerston Library featuring Holly Gramazio and Jim Munroe discussing the intersection of writing and games.


(photo: Marie LeBlanc Flanagan)

Team Up Activated brought Marvel Rivals tournaments to Invictus Game Station.

(photo: Kevin Ly)

The Sound of Soft Rains' New Sci-Fi Game at the Canadian Music Centre offered a behind-the-scenes look at video game music creation.


(photo: T Nguyen)

The IGDA Game Programmers Roundtable at George Brown College brought together veteran programmers to share career insights and advice.


(photo: Pablo Calatayud)

Monday ended with the joyful Parachute Club in Christie Pits Park, where people of all ages came together to invent games in a giant rainbow parachute.

(photo: kreztakingphotos)

Tuesday, June 17: Art and Preservation


Tuesday started with Sherwin Tjia's SUNCHASER at InterAccess, where participants took turns flying a flight simulator while chasing an endless sunset projected on the walls.


(photo: Artbears)

The Portable Preservation showcase at the Royal Ontario Museum celebrated handheld gaming history.


(photo: Marie LeBlanc Flanagan)

We gathered for microtalks at Bonus Stage at TacoTaco.

(photo: Bonus Stage)

And travelled onwards to Seriously Fun at Danu Social House, dedicated to games for change, educational games, and serious game development.

(photo: Denis of Werocreative)

Wednesday, June 18

We wrapped it all up with the Toronto Games Week Closing Party at Trinity Square Video with OMNIVOX, a magical cube with 4,096 LEDs and a custom playful experience designed by Callum Hay, Sara Vinten, and Jessica Mak.

(photo: Kevin Ly)
